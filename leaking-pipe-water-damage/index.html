Leaking Pipe Water Damage Help in Lynchburg

You seldom catch a pipe leak the day it begins. The drip is hidden in a wall cavity, a sink cabinet or beneath the slab, so the first clue tends to be a stain, a spongy patch of floor or an oddly high water bill. At that point the drywall and wood nearby may have been soaking for weeks. Our team in Lynchburg finds how far the moisture has spread and dries all of it, not just the part you can see.

Fixing the pipe ends the leak, yet the moisture already inside the wall or floor stays put. This is where our team comes in. Our crew maps the damp materials with thermal imaging and moisture meters, opens up no more than necessary, and runs dehumidifiers and air movers until the readings return to normal. Because slow leaks give mold time to start, we also check for growth and treat affected surfaces.

First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Stop the Water SupplyFor a leak at a toilet or sink, turn off the small supply valve feeding that fixture. If you cannot find the source, turn off the main shutoff valve for the house.
  2. Stay Clear of ElectricityTurn off the breaker serving the area when water is close to a light fixture, switch or outlet. Leave any wet cord or electrical device alone.
  3. Read the MeterShut off all taps and fixtures, then go look at the water meter. Movement on the dial means water is escaping out of sight, perhaps beneath the slab.
  4. Empty and PhotographBefore a repair is made or anything goes in the trash, remove what is stored near the leak and get pictures of the pipe, the staining and the warping.
  5. Have the Area MeteredHave the area measured with meters before it is closed up. Inside the cavity, moisture can remain even when the wall surface feels dry.
Coverage

Insurance and Leaking Pipe Water Damage

Insurers draw a line between a sudden leak and a slow one. Sudden and accidental damage is often covered, but damage from a leak that went on for weeks or months usually is not, and some policies exclude it by name. An adjuster may look more kindly on a leak hidden from view than on one that could be seen and was left alone. Check your policy and ask your agent. We document the source of the leak, moisture readings and photos so the adjuster has clear facts.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • Water pressure set too high, which wears on every joint and valve until a slow weep begins.
  • Hidden joints, soldered or crimped, that fail behind walls and above ceilings.
  • Older copper pipe that corrodes from the inside until a pinhole opens in the wall of the line.
  • Supply connections and compression fittings behind toilets or under sinks that have worn out or worked loose.
  • A nail or screw driven into a pipe during a remodel or while hanging something on the wall.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Ceiling and Drywall Damage

A pipe leaking above a ceiling or inside a wall leaves brown rings and bubbled paint. Drywall that stays wet goes soft, sags and may need to be cut out.

Wet Insulation

Insulation in the wall soaks up leak water like a sponge and holds it against the framing. It dries very slowly in a closed cavity, so saturated insulation is usually pulled out.

Swollen Cabinets

Particleboard cabinet bases under a sink swell and crumble once they absorb water. Swollen board stays swollen, and after a lengthy leak the base often has to be replaced.

Mold Inside the Wall

Nothing suits mold better than the dark, moist space inside a wall. During a slow leak, the back side of the drywall can be covered well before the painted face shows one spot.
